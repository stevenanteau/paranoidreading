Gladio and the Strategy of Tension

The best documented case of a democratic state keeping a secret army, and what it became after the Cold War ended.

0 / 115 read

Operation Gladio is the paradigm case, and it is the reason this site does not treat parapolitics as a synonym for conspiracism. The stay behind networks are not alleged. Giulio Andreotti confirmed them to the Italian parliament in 1990, the European Parliament passed a resolution on them that November, and the Italian and Belgian inquiries put the documents into the public record. What remains genuinely argued among historians is narrower and more interesting: how much operational control the networks had over specific massacres, and how far the same apparatus ran outside Italy.

That distinction is the whole method. A dossier is useful only if it marks where the record ends and the inference starts. Every holding below carries a grade saying what kind of claim it is making, and the sections move from the strongest evidence outward: the core account, then the peer reviewed scholarship, then the contemporary press, then the primary documents, then the material in other languages that the Anglophone literature mostly ignores.

The second half of the dossier is an argument rather than a settled history. The stay behind model was a state keeping deniable violence on retainer. That arrangement did not end in 1990, it was privatised and it was financed. The Erik Prince material and the Asian narco network material are here because they are the same structure under new management: force for hire, commerce that needs it, and a state that benefits while denying the connection. Read that section as a hypothesis the sources are testing, not as a conclusion.

How this dossier is graded

Every holding carries a grade for what kind of claim it makes, not for how good it is. Quality ratings are the Noided and Grim system, which lives on the book pages.

Primary document. Parliamentary papers, court filings, archives. The record itself, not an account of it.

Peer reviewed. Published in a scholarly venue and refereed. The claim has survived specialists.

Documented. Non fiction resting on the public record. Where specialists dispute part of it, the dispute is named.

Reportage. Journalism. Accurate as of filing, provisional by nature, and in the modern sections it is still moving.

Contested. The central claim is argued seriously on both sides. Read it with the counter argument in hand.

Disputed. Substantially challenged, thinly sourced, or written by someone with an interest in the story. Kept as part of the literature, not as evidence.

Movement publication. Written from inside a political current for that current. Valuable as position and as primary material, not as neutral reporting.

Dramatization. Fiction built on documented events, naming real people or operations. The events are real, the scenes are invented.

Fiction. Invented. Here for what it makes thinkable.

Unidentified. A file in the library whose source cannot currently be established. Do not cite it.

A holding marked citation reconstructed was in the library as a bare file. The citation was rebuilt by hand and has not been verified against the physical item. One marked citation incomplete is listed exactly as the library holds it.
