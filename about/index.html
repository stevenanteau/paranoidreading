Paranoid Reading

A lens for reading the world. Dialectical materialism, capital as cosmic horror, and the parapolitics of deep events, with fiction and non-fiction given equal weight.

Paranoid Reading is a way of looking, not a genre. It starts from a materialist premise: history is made by forces of production, by class, and by the institutions built to protect accumulation, and those forces do much of their work where nobody is meant to look. The paranoia here is disciplined. The question is never who is pulling the strings. It is whose interests an arrangement serves, and what it has to hide to keep serving them.

I

Dialectical materialism

Begin with the material base. Read events as the working out of contradictions between classes, between capital and labour, between a state's public face and its interests. Not great men and not secret masters, but structures with motives, and the people who staff them.

II

Capital as cosmic horror

Lovecraft's terror is the discovery that the universe is run by vast, indifferent powers the human mind was not built to perceive. That is a fair description of capital: a non human process, older than anyone living, that reorganises bodies, landscapes and states to feed itself and is only ever glimpsed in fragments. Weird fiction gives us words for what economics calls the weather.

III

Parapolitics and deep events

The covert record: stay behind armies, intelligence agencies in the drug trade, assassinations, the narco netherworld, the private armies that replaced them. Deep events are the ones the official story cannot explain without the hidden layer. They are researched here through inquiries, archives and scholarship, and every source is graded for what it can bear.

Fiction as conception

The novel often arrives first. De Maria wrote the strategy of tension before the archives opened, Ellroy made the covert sixties legible, Pynchon mapped the cartel. Fiction lets us conceive what the record later confirms, and warns us when the record itself is being dramatised.

Non-fiction as reality

The record tells us what can be shown. Parliamentary papers, peer reviewed work, reportage, testimony, each marked for the kind of claim it makes. A reading on this site always holds both: the novel that imagines the machine and the document that proves it exists.

How entries are graded

Every entry carries a grade for what kind of claim it makes, not for how good it is.

Primary document
Parliamentary papers, court filings, archives. The record itself, not an account of it.
Primary text
A text read as a source in its own right: scripture, manifestos, the right's own books.
Peer reviewed
Published in a scholarly venue and refereed. The claim has survived specialists.
Documented
Non fiction resting on the public record. Where specialists dispute part of it, the dispute is named.
Theory
Philosophy and political theory. An argument to think with, not a record of events.
Reportage
Journalism. Accurate as of filing, provisional by nature, and in the modern sections it is still moving.
Contested
The central claim is argued seriously on both sides. Read it with the counter argument in hand.
Disputed
Substantially challenged, thinly sourced, or written by someone with an interest in the story. Kept as part of the literature, not as evidence.
Movement publication
Written from inside a political current for that current. Valuable as position and as primary material, not as neutral reporting.
Dramatization
Fiction built on documented events, naming real people or operations. The events are real, the scenes are invented.
Fiction
Invented. Here for what it makes thinkable.
